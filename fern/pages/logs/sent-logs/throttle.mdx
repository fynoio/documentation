When Fyno holds a notification back instead of submitting it to the provider straight away, the log records it. The Trace flags the notification as throttled, and a **Throttling details** panel lists every delayed attempt with its timestamp.

Use it when a notification reaches the recipient later than expected and you need to know whether the delay happened inside Fyno, before the provider was ever called.

**Where to find it:** open a log → **Log Details** → **Trace** → the **Sent to** _&lt;provider name&gt;_ step.

## The throttling indicator on the Trace

The indicator sits on the **Sent to** _&lt;provider name&gt;_ step, just below Fyno's Processing Time. It appears only when the notification was actually throttled. A notification submitted without delay shows no indicator.

![](/images/sent-logs-throttling-trace.png)

| Element | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Throttling Enabled** | Fyno delayed submitting this notification to the provider instead of sending it immediately. |
| **View Details** | Opens the Throttling details panel for this notification. |
| **Fyno's Processing Time** | The time Fyno took to process the request before handing it to the provider. |

## The Throttling details panel

Select **View Details** to open the panel. It lists every attempt Fyno made to submit the notification, in the order they happened.

| Element | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Panel heading** | The total number of submission attempts, shown as _N Attempts_. |
| **Delay rows** | One row per attempt, labelled _1st delay_, _2nd delay_ and so on, in chronological order. |
| **Date and time** | The timestamp recorded against each delayed attempt, in DD-MM-YYYY \| HH:MM:SS format. |

In the example below, Fyno held the notification across 9 attempts before submitting it, each with its own timestamp.

![](/images/sent-logs-throttling-details-panel.png)

## Reading the panel

- **Total hold time.** The gap between the first delay and the timestamp on the **Sent** step is how long Fyno held the notification before it went out.
- **Spacing of attempts.** Attempts clustered within a few seconds point to a short burst of contention. Attempts spread over minutes point to sustained rate limiting.
- **Scope of the problem.** A high attempt count on one log is worth checking against other logs from the same send. If the whole batch is throttled, the limit is too low for the volume; the individual notification is not at fault.
- **Outcome.** Throttling delays a notification; it does not fail it. A throttled notification that is eventually submitted still moves to **Sent** and **Delivered** as normal.

## When throttling applies

You can set throttling on a campaign. It is optional but recommended, and controls how fast a campaign sends notifications on Email, SMS, WhatsApp, Push and Web Push. You can throttle a campaign by time (how long it runs) or by batch (how many records go out per interval). The throttle limit set in your provider account can slow delivery further.

To set it up, see [How to throttle a campaign](https://www.fyno.io/docs/campaign_creation#how-to-throttle-a-campaign).
