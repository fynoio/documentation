AMP (Accelerated Mobile Pages) for Email lets you send dynamic, interactive content inside the email itself, such as carousels, accordions and forms. Recipients can interact with the content without opening a new tab or visiting a website, which gives them a website-like experience right in their inbox.

<Tip>
In Fyno, the [Stripo editor](./template-creation-email#stripo-editor) supports AMP. The AMP blocks (**Carousel**, **Accordion** and **Form**) are marked with a lightning icon in the **Blocks** section.
</Tip>

## What you can do with AMP emails

<CardGroup cols={3}>
<Card title="Forms" icon="fa-solid fa-square-check">
Run surveys and collect feedback, such as NPS and CSAT, without the user leaving the email.
</Card>
<Card title="Carousels" icon="fa-solid fa-images">
Present content with many links in a carousel that users can browse without opening a new page.
</Card>
<Card title="Accordions" icon="fa-solid fa-bars-staggered">
Show details of multiple products or services in collapsible sections, so users open only what interests them.
</Card>
</CardGroup>

## How AMP for Email works

A marketing or transactional email is usually a multipart message, with each part delivered as a different MIME type:

  <table style={tableStyle} className="fyno-styled-table">
    <thead>
      <tr>
        <th style={thStyle}>Part</th>
        <th style={thStyle}>What it is</th>
      </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr>
        <td style={tdStyle}><strong>Plain text</strong></td>
        <td style={tdStyle}>The original email format. It still acts as a fallback for the HTML part.</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td style={tdStyle}><strong>HTML</strong></td>
        <td style={tdStyle}>The format most email clients support today.</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td style={tdStyle}><strong>AMP HTML</strong></td>
        <td style={tdStyle}>AMP markup, which looks like HTML but follows its own specification. It is delivered as a third MIME type, on top of HTML and plain text.</td>
      </tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

The AMP part is shown only in inboxes that support AMP. All other email clients fall back to the HTML version.

## Before you send AMP emails

To send AMP emails, you need to register as a sender with the email clients that support AMP.

<Steps>
<Step title="Set up SPF and DKIM">

Make sure SPF (Sender Policy Framework) and DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) are set up for the domain you send from.

</Step>
<Step title="Get approved for dynamic emails">

Your email must be approved for sending AMP (dynamic) emails, including elements like carousels, accordions and forms. See [Google's AMP email documentation](https://developers.google.com/gmail/ampemail).

</Step>
<Step title="Send a production-ready AMP email for review">

Send a production-ready AMP email to these addresses:

- `ampverification@yahoo.com`
- `ampforemail.whitelisting@gmail.com`
- `postmaster_amp@corp.mail.ru` (recommended only if you have more than 5,000 users with the mail.ru domain)

</Step>
<Step title="Complete the sender registration form">

Fill in the [AMP for Email: Sender Registration form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSdso95e7UDLk_R-bnpzsAmuUMDQEMUgTErcfGGItBDkghHU2A/viewform?gxids=7628). This is the only form you need to fill in to send AMP emails to all supporting email clients.

</Step>
<Step title="Wait for confirmation">

Each email client tells you your allowlist status by email. If you have any issues, contact the AMP for Email Working Group through the [AMP for Email GitHub repository](https://github.com/ampproject/wg-amp4email).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning title="Important">
- Register every From email address you plan to send AMP emails from.
- Adding your From email address to Gmail's allowlist can take up to three weeks.
- Yahoo Mail doesn't send a confirmation email. After 7 days, AMP starts working in Yahoo Mail if you meet all its sender requirements.
</Warning>

### Other requirements

- The recipient must have dynamic emails enabled in their inbox settings.
- You must send a non-AMP fallback email along with the AMP version, so the email still renders in inboxes that don't support AMP or have it disabled. See [HTML fallback for AMP emails](#html-fallback-for-amp-emails).

<Tip title="Test before you're allowlisted">
You can test AMP emails by sending them to yourself. In Gmail, go to **Settings → Dynamic email → Developer settings** and add the email address that sends the dynamic content.
</Tip>

## HTML fallback for AMP emails

Always create an HTML fallback for your AMP emails, so every recipient has a good experience. This matters most when you add videos or images, which some email clients can't show.

The recipient sees the fallback instead of the AMP version when:

      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td style={tdStyle}><strong>The email is forwarded</strong></td>

      <tr>

      </tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

In all these cases, the HTML fallback makes sure the recipient still gets the information they need.
