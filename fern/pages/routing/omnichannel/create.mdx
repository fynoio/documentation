You build Omnichannel Routes in Fyno's Omnichannel Route Builder by placing channels and controls on a canvas and connecting them.

## Create an Omnichannel Route

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Log in to your Fyno account and go to **Routes → Omni-Channel** in the left navigation. This page lists your existing omnichannel routes, with each route's description and when it was last edited. Use the search bar and the **Version** filter to find a route. To manage an existing route, see [Manage your routes](./omnichannel-routing-introduction#manage-your-routes).

    ![](/images/omni-channel-routes-listing.png)
  </Step>
  <Step>Click **+ Create**.</Step>
  <Step>
    In the **Create Omni-channel route** dialog, enter a **Name** for the route and, optionally, a **Description**. Click **Submit**.

    ![](/images/create-omni-channel-route-dialog.png)
  </Step>
  <Step>
    The route builder opens with your route at `v1 (Test)` and a **Start** node on the canvas. The components you build the route with are on the left.

    ![](/images/omni-channel-route-builder.png)
  </Step>
  <Step>Drag components onto the canvas and connect them, starting from the **Start** node. Each component is explained below.</Step>
  <Step>Click **Save** to save your changes to the test version.</Step>
  <Step>
    When the route is ready, click **Go Live**, then click **Confirm** in the **Go Live Confirmation** dialog.

    ![](/images/go-live-confirmation.png)

    <Note>
      If approvals are turned on for your workspace, clicking **Go Live** asks you to request approval instead. See [Approval Requests](./approval-request) for how this works.
    </Note>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip title="Proximity matters!">
  The builder has proximity connectors: a component you drop on the canvas automatically connects to the closest component that has a free connection node.
</Tip>

## Channels

The top section of the components pane lists the channels: **SMS**, **WhatsApp**, **Email**, **Slack**, **Discord**, **Teams**, **Push**, **InApp**, **Voice** and **RCS**.

Click a channel node on the canvas to configure it:

- **Provider/Route** (required): Choose from the providers you have added in **Integrations** for that channel, or from the single channel routes you have created for it. If no integration is configured for the channel, the list is empty.
- **Success Criteria** (optional): Define which statuses count as success, and how long to wait for them.
  - **Wait For** and **Unit**: How long the route waits for a status after sending.
  - **for**: The statuses that count as success. You can select more than one, and receiving any one of them counts as success. The statuses available depend on the channel. For SMS, they are Delivered, Clicked, Invalid-Sub, Failed, Rejected, Undelivered, Dropped and No Report.

![](/images/channel-node-settings.png)

![](/images/success-criteria-statuses.png)

Once configured, the node summarizes its setup, for example: *Send SMS through [provider], then wait 1 minute for Success Criteria*.

For example, to treat a WhatsApp notification as successful when it is delivered or read within 2 minutes of being sent:

1. Turn on **Success Criteria**.
2. Set **Wait For** to `2` and **Unit** to **Minutes**.
3. Select the statuses, such as **Delivered** or **Read**.

The route then waits up to 2 minutes for either status. If one is received, the route follows the success flow. If neither is received within 2 minutes, it follows the failure flow.

<Info>The wait time can be from a minimum of 5 seconds to a maximum of 24 hours.</Info>

<Tip>To end the flow at a channel, turn off **Success Criteria** for that channel.</Tip>

## Status-based routing

Every channel node with Success Criteria turned on has two outputs, **Success** and **Failure**:

- **Success**: The flow connected here runs when the notification receives one of the selected statuses within the wait time.
- **Failure**: The flow connected here runs when the notification does not receive any of the selected statuses within the wait time.

In the route below, an SMS is sent first, with a 1-minute wait for its Success Criteria. On success, a WhatsApp message is sent. On failure, an Email is sent. If the WhatsApp message fails, a **Condition** decides the next step: if it is true, an Email is sent, and if it is false, an InApp notification is sent.

## Controls

Controls shape the direction your route takes. There are two controls.

### Condition

A **Condition** runs different actions depending on whether the condition is met. It has two outputs, **True** and **False**. For example, to choose the channel based on user type: if the user is a Power User, send WhatsApp, otherwise send SMS.

To set up a condition, fill in:

- **Condition**: What to check, for example **Destination** or **Country**.
- **Channel**: The channel the check applies to.
- **Operator**: How to compare, for example **equals** or **in**.
- **Value**: The value or values to compare against.

Click **Add Another** to add more conditions. Multiple conditions are always combined with **AND**, so all of them must be met for the condition to be true. Click the delete icon to remove a condition.

![](/images/condition-settings.png)

### Wait For

**Wait For** adds a delay to the route, so you can stack your notifications without bombarding users across all channels at once. Set:

- **Enter Time**: The length of the delay.
- **Unit**: The unit of time for the delay.

<Tip>
  A delay can be at most 24 hours, which means you can enter:
  <ol>
    <li>Up to 1440 minutes</li>
    <li>Up to 86400 seconds</li>
  </ol>
</Tip>

## When a destination is missing

If a user's destination for a channel is missing, that step is automatically treated as a failure, and the step connected to its **Failure** output runs.

For example, say a route sends an SMS, then sends an Email on success or a WhatsApp message on failure. If the user has no SMS destination, the WhatsApp message is sent, as defined in the failure flow.
