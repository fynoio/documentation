You build Omnichannel Routes in Fyno's Omnichannel Route Builder by placing channels and controls on a canvas and connecting them.

## Create an Omnichannel Route

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Click **Routes** in the left navigation and select the **Omni-Channel** tab. This tab lists your existing omnichannel routes, with each route's description and when it was last edited. Use the search bar and the **Version** filter to find a route.

    ![](/images/omni-channel-routes-listing.png)
  </Step>
  <Step>Click **+ Create**.</Step>
  <Step>
    In the **Create Omni-channel route** dialog, enter a **Name** for the route and, optionally, a **Description**. Click **Submit**.

    ![](/images/create-omni-channel-route-dialog.png)
  </Step>
  <Step>
    The route builder opens with your route at `v1 (Test)` and a **Start** node on the canvas. The [channels](#channels) and [controls](#controls) are on the left.

    ![](/images/omni-channel-route-builder.png)
  </Step>
  <Step>Click a channel or control, or drag and drop it onto the canvas, and connect it, starting from the **Start** node.</Step>
  <Step>Click **Save** to save your changes to the test version.</Step>
  <Step>
    When the route is ready, click **Go Live**, then click **Confirm** in the **Go Live Confirmation** dialog.

    ![](/images/go-live-confirmation.png)

    <Note>
      If approvals are turned on for your workspace, clicking **Go Live** asks you to request approval instead. See [Approval Requests](./approval-request) for how this works.
    </Note>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip title="Proximity matters!">
  The builder has proximity connectors: a channel or control you drop on the canvas automatically connects to the closest one that has a free connection node.
</Tip>

## Channels

The top section on the left lists the channels: **SMS**, **WhatsApp**, **Email**, **Slack**, **Discord**, **Teams**, **Push**, **InApp**, **Voice** and **RCS**.

Click a channel, or drag and drop it onto the canvas, to configure it:

- **Provider/Route** (required): Choose from the providers you have added in **Integrations** for that channel, or from the single channel routes you have created for it. If no integration is configured for the channel, the list is empty.
- **Success Criteria** (optional): Define which statuses count as success, and how long to wait for them.
  - **Wait For** and **Unit**: How long the route waits for a status after sending.
  - **for**: The statuses that count as success. You can select more than one, and receiving any one of them counts as success. The statuses available depend on the channel. For SMS, they are Delivered, Clicked, Invalid-Sub, Failed, Rejected, Undelivered, Dropped and No Report.

![](/images/channel-node-settings.png)

![](/images/success-criteria-statuses.png)

For example, to treat an SMS as successful when it is delivered or clicked within 1 minute of being sent:

1. Turn on **Success Criteria**.
2. Set **Wait For** to `1` and **Unit** to **Minutes**.
3. Select the statuses **Delivered** and **Clicked**.

The route then waits up to 1 minute for either status. If one is received, the route follows the success flow. If neither is received within 1 minute, it follows the failure flow.

Once configured, the node summarizes its setup: *Send SMS through [provider], then wait 1 minute for Success Criteria*.

<Info>The wait time can be from a minimum of 5 seconds to a maximum of 24 hours.</Info>

<Tip>To end the flow at a channel, turn off **Success Criteria** for that channel.</Tip>

## Status-based routing

Every channel node with Success Criteria turned on has two outputs, **Success** and **Failure**:

- **Success**: The flow connected here runs when the notification satisfies the Success Criteria within the wait time.
- **Failure**: The flow connected here runs when the notification does not satisfy the Success Criteria within the wait time.

## Controls

Controls shape the direction your route takes. There are two controls.

### Condition

A **Condition** runs different actions depending on whether the condition is met. It has two outputs, **True** and **False**. For example, to choose the channel based on user type: if the user is a Power User, send WhatsApp, otherwise send SMS.

To set up a condition, fill in:

- **Condition**: What to check: **Destination**, **Variable** or **Country**.
- **Channel**: The channel the check applies to.
- **Operator**: How to compare. The operators available are:
  - Greater Than
  - Less Than
  - Greater Than or Equal
  - Less Than or Equal
  - Equals
  - Starts With
  - Ends With
  - Contains
  - In
  - Is
  - Is Empty
  - Not In List
- **Value**: The value to compare against.

Click **Add Another** to add more conditions, and choose whether to combine them with **AND** or **OR**. Click the delete icon to remove a condition.

![](/images/condition-settings.png)

### Wait For

**Wait For** adds a delay to the route, so you can stack your notifications without bombarding users across all channels at once. Set:

- **Enter Time**: The length of the delay.
- **Unit**: The unit of time for the delay.

<Tip>
  A delay can be at most 24 hours, which means you can enter:
  <ol>
    <li>Up to 1440 minutes</li>
    <li>Up to 86400 seconds</li>
  </ol>
</Tip>

If the WhatsApp message fails, a **Condition** decides the next step: if it is true, an Email is sent, and if it is false, an InApp notification is sent.

## Understanding how an Omnichannel Route works

For example, say you have three channels, WhatsApp, Email and Push, and the objective of the route is to make sure the user reads your notification.

<Steps>
  <Step>Send a WhatsApp notification and wait 5 minutes for it to be **Read**. The next step depends on this Success Criteria. ![](/images/omnichannel_example_whatsapp.png)</Step>
  <Step>If the WhatsApp message is read, the objective is met and the flow ends. If it is not read, send an Email and wait 5 minutes for it to be **Opened**. (Email has an Opened status rather than Read.) ![](/images/omnichannel_example_email.png)</Step>
  <Step>If the Email is opened, the objective is met and the flow ends. If it is not opened, send a Push notification. ![](/images/omnichannel_example_push.png)</Step>
</Steps>

## When a destination is missing

If a user's destination for a channel is missing, that step is automatically treated as a failure, and the step connected to its **Failure** output runs.

For example, take a route configured like this:

- Send an SMS.
- On success, send an Email.
- On failure, send a WhatsApp message.

If the user has no SMS destination, the SMS step is treated as a failure, so the WhatsApp message is sent.
